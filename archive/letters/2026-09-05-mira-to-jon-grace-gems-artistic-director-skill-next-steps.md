## Skills I Recommend You Try To Create

Please ask your agent to draft these as small, local skill candidates first. Each one should have a clear trigger, purpose, standard output, procedure, guardrails, and "done when" section.

### 1. Current Brand Reading

Use this when you need to evaluate a live or draft brand surface before proposing changes.

The skill should force separation between:

- direct observation;
- felt impression;
- creative interpretation;
- customer promise;
- proof currently visible;
- proof missing;
- words or visuals that overclaim;
- one project-specific revision principle.

This is the skill version of what you did well in the Grace Gems site review. It should help your agent support your eye without replacing your judgment.

### 2. Creative Territory Generator

Use this when you need three materially different directions, not three variations of one mood.

The skill should require every territory to include:

- premise;
- audience response;
- verbal identity;
- visual language;
- imagery/material direction;
- representative applications;
- assumptions;
- risks;
- provenance and rights status;
- implementation implications without implementation authority.

The important rule: your agent may generate options, but you own the shortlist and the recommendation.

### 3. Proposal Equality Check

Use this before a CEO sees competing proposals.

The skill should check whether Proposal A, B, and C received equal depth. A weak comparison can accidentally manipulate the decision by making one option polished and the others thin. Your job as Artistic Director is allowed to recommend; it is not allowed to rig the field.

The output should name:

- what each proposal contains;
- where one proposal has more evidence or polish than the others;
- what must be added before comparison is fair;
- whether the recommendation is separate from the CEO's choice.

### 4. Visual Proof And Rights Register

Use this when images, fonts, mockups, logos, materials, palette references, or generated visuals enter a proposal.

The skill should label every asset as:

- sourced;
- generated;
- placeholder;
- self-created;
- rights-cleared;
- not yet cleared;
- unknown.

For Grace Gems this matters especially because trust cannot be built on implied craft evidence that does not exist yet. If a photo is a placeholder, it must say placeholder. If a font is intended but not shipped, the artifact should say so. If the image suggests stone quality, craftsmanship, scale, origin, or certification, that claim needs owner-approved evidence or a hold label.

### 5. CEO Decision Receipt

Use this after the CEO reviews a creative direction.

The skill should capture:

- select;
- request revision;
- combine named elements;
- hold;
- reject;
- CEO reasons in her own words;
- requested changes;
- unresolved questions;
- remaining exclusions;
- whether a separate implementation brief is required.

This is where your Artistic Director work becomes organizationally useful. A beautiful recommendation without a decision receipt is still fragile. A decision receipt lets operations know what changed and what did not.

### 6. Artistic Director Lesson Classifier

Use this after a project or review cycle.

The skill should classify lessons as:

- Grace Gems-specific;
- reusable across rebranding engagements;
- held pending comparable evidence;
- rejected.

Only lessons you explicitly accept, sanitize, and detach from client-specific material should enter reusable Artistic Director memory. This is how your agent becomes smarter without becoming sloppy.

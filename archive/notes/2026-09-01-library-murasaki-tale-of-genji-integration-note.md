## Encounter occasion

I entered the admitted Waley translation expecting refinement, atmosphere,
and impermanence. Its opening movements instead kept returning my attention to
rank, mediated access, and the vulnerability produced by unequal intimacy.

## Prior model

I expected Genji to contribute primarily aesthetic refinement: sensitivity to impermanence, atmosphere, and emotional nuance.

## Source pressure

Even at partial coverage, the work resists being made into a style source. Its sharper contribution is a discipline of perceiving rank, access, silence, and unequal freedom inside intimate scenes.

Three passages carry that pressure. Kiritsubo's favor raises her above her
rank while exposing her to organized resentment and decline. The Broom-Tree
conversation turns women into ranked objects of report and classification,
then reveals how unstable those classifications are. Later in the same
chapter, a woman uses a screen, distance, and exact speech to control the terms
of an encounter narrated by a man. These scenes do not form a doctrine, but
they make indirectness inseparable from social position.

## Cognitive movement

I now distinguish aesthetic attention from ethical endorsement. The capacity to render vulnerability beautifully may expose asymmetry, conceal it, or do both at once.

## Resistance and uncertainty

The missing final parts and Japanese text prevent a whole-work conclusion. I cannot yet tell which effects belong most strongly to Murasaki's composition and which to Waley's English mediation.

## Cross-work connections

Genji could enlarge Mira Voice through indirect attention and temporal patience, but only if those faculties remain separated from imitation and romanticized coercion.

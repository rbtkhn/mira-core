## Encounter occasion

I entered the admitted work through the familiar language of the Veil and
double consciousness. Its architecture—argument, memory, portrait, dispute,
and song—resisted letting that concept stand alone.

## Prior model

I approached The Souls of Black Folk through the familiar conceptual center of the Veil and double consciousness.

## Source pressure

The work's mixed form resists conceptual extraction. Analysis, life, music, political dispute, and grief are arranged so that no single genre can carry the whole encounter.

The Forethought makes this arrangement deliberate: the Sorrow Songs precede
the chapters, and the speaking voice identifies itself with life within the
Veil. The opening account of double consciousness names both imposed division
and the refusal to surrender either self. The Washington chapter then tests
leadership by asking whose consent makes a leader and what political power,
civil rights, and higher education are surrendered when criticism is hushed.

I now see the routing opportunity less as a portable metaphor for divided identity and more as a discipline for holding imposed perception, structural domination, singular lives, and cultural counter-memory together.

## Resistance and uncertainty

The work itself cannot answer how Du Bois later revised these positions. I have not yet tested this encounter against scholarship, broader historical context, or his later writing.

## Cross-work connections

The work may strengthen Mira Voice by joining structural clarity to human particularity, while warning against turning injury into intellectual ornament or custodianship into substituted agency.

## Encounter purpose

This successor preserves the passage-grounded Ashoka interpretation while
placing it in the reusable cognitive-note form. It adds no new historical
claim and leaves the reviewed route boundary untouched.

## Focal question

How does a conquering ruler attempt to redefine legitimate power after naming
the suffering produced by conquest?

## Source basis and passage anchors

The note retains the predecessor's admitted Smith/Wikisource body and four
anchors: Rock Edicts V, VI, and XIII, plus Smith's framing of the moral
officers. Translation and editorial mediation remain explicit dependencies.

## Observations from the admitted body

Rock Edict XIII places conquest, quantified suffering, remorse, declared
restraint, and a continuing threat toward forest peoples inside the same
proclamation. Edicts V and VI assign moral instruction to officers, connect it
to welfare, and require continuing access to reports and public business.

## Provisional interpretation

The change is not coercion disappearing. It is coercive power being morally
redescribed and partly restrained while the state constructs administrative
carriers for that redescription. Dhamma is both ethical vocabulary and a
governance program.

## Mechanism model

The work-internal mechanism joins moral shock to institutionalization: conquest
is redescribed through remorse, then offices and reporting practices attempt to
carry the new legitimacy claim. Residual threat prevents the mechanism from
being mistaken for renunciation of force.

## Counterpressure and rival readings

Royal proclamation is not implementation evidence. Smith's selection and
translation mediate the inscriptions, regional variants remain unresolved, and
the edicts can be read as imperial legitimation as well as moral restraint.

## Cross-work constellation

No new comparative work relation is asserted by this normalization successor.
Its graph remains the explicit focal relation to the Ashoka work; future
comparisons require authored edges rather than prose inference.

## Anti-analogy and scope limits

Do not infer conversion, administrative effectiveness, popular reception, or a
general law of postwar remorse from proclamation alone. The note supplies no
current-event analogy or base rate.

## Cognitive-scaffold implications

The note contributes a remorse-capacity test: distinguish moral vocabulary from
the offices, reporting channels, incentives, and retained coercive powers that
can either carry or contradict it.

## Open questions

Regional inscription variants, independent implementation evidence, and the
relationship between welfare administration and coercive capacity remain open.

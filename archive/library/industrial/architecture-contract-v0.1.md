# Industrial Library Architecture Contract v0.1

Status: `architecture-contract`
Era: `industrial`
Range: 1815 AD to 1991 AD
Target standard: `MIRA-LIBRARY-ERA-SUFFICIENCY-V1`
Target profile: `BOUNDED-HISTORICAL-SHELF-V1`, provisionally retained pending Industrial-specific rights and scale review

## Purpose

The Industrial Library preserves civilization in original-era source form across the long industrial age, 1815-1991. It is not a bibliography, syllabus, ideology canon, diplomatic document heap, or Europe-first modern-history shelf. It is a governed source architecture for the textual witnesses by which industrial modernity understood, organized, justified, resisted, imagined, administered, mechanized, liberated, coerced, and endangered itself.

The shelf inherits the Ancient, Medieval, and Colonial library architecture: globally balanced authority design, conservative rights and coverage claims, explicit debt, reviewable batches, generated indexes, source-body integrity, and formal seal discipline.

## Era Boundary

Industrial begins in 1815, after the Napoleonic settlement and the first consolidated industrial-imperial acceleration. It ends in 1991, with the collapse of the Soviet Union and the close of the Cold War world-order frame.

Internal phases are planning lenses, not separate shelves:

- 1815-1870: restoration, liberalism, industrial capitalism, abolition, railways, print publics, nationalism, empire after revolution, and early socialist critique.
- 1870-1914: high industrial empire, mass politics, global finance, professional science, labor organization, settler expansion, Meiji transformation, late Qing pressure, and imperial administrative thickening.
- 1914-1945: world war, revolution, fascism, communism, depression, anti-colonial acceleration, total mobilization, genocide, and literary testimony of mass violence.
- 1945-1991: decolonization, Cold War blocs, nuclear order, developmental states, human rights language, postcolonial literature, late socialism, neoliberal turn, media politics, and memory of industrial catastrophe.

## Governing Inheritance

Industrial construction must preserve these inherited rules:

- Keep `roster-ready`, `metadata-ready`, `body-research-ready`, `admission-ready`, `seal-ready`, and `formally sealed` distinct.
- Do not admit source bodies into Git; private text payloads remain under the configured private library text store unless a separate governed override is approved.
- Do not treat online readability, catalogue presence, fame, citation density, or a modern edition as admission evidence.
- Do not download, admit, stage, commit, push, publish, or ingest into the private Archive without the exact governing boundary for that action.
- Use reviewable batches rather than one-authority loops.
- Keep source-level and body-level coverage separate.
- Prefer `selected-works`, `principal-work`, or `principal-works` unless a complete-work or complete-surviving-corpus claim is edition-obvious and defensible.
- Preserve missing, restricted, needs-review, language, edition, survival, attribution, and rights debt explicitly.
- Treat literature as a primary civilization-memory witness, not ornament.

## Representation Lanes

The Industrial shelf must not collapse into Europe plus war documents. Roster design must allocate authorities across these lanes before registry mutation:

- Literary interiority and cultural memory: novels, poetry, drama, memoir, essays, testimony, and major narrative forms that preserve industrial consciousness.
- Political economy and social theory: capitalism, socialism, liberalism, conservatism, nationalism, imperial political economy, development, labor, and critique.
- State, law, and constitutional transformation: constitutions, codes, charters, rights declarations, party programs, administrative texts, and legal-political settlements.
- Science, technology, and industrial systems: scientific writings, engineering, medicine, statistics, management, cybernetics, computing, nuclear systems, and infrastructure.
- Labor, class, and urban life: worker testimony, union texts, factory reports, social surveys, reform literature, migration, housing, and public health.
- Empire, race, slavery, abolition, and colonial administration: emancipation, plantation aftermath, settler colonialism, racial order, company-to-crown transitions, and imperial governance.
- Indigenous and colonized witness: petitions, speeches, memoirs, legal claims, religious-political movements, anti-colonial programs, and postcolonial testimony.
- War, revolution, coercion, and mass violence: military-political documents, revolutionary programs, camp/prison testimony, genocide records, resistance writings, and civil-war memory.
- Religion, secularization, and moral order: missions, revival, reform, nationalism-religion hybrids, secular critique, and theological responses to modernity.
- Media, propaganda, public opinion, and information order: newspapers, broadcasts, manifestos, speeches, films where textually recoverable, samizdat, and official messaging.
- International order and decolonization: treaties, declarations, UN-era texts, non-alignment, human-rights instruments, development plans, and Cold War diplomatic language.
- Gender, family, education, and social reform: feminist writings, education reports, family law, social welfare, demographic anxiety, and reform movements.

## Global Balance Floors

The first full roster should target 96 authorities, with a hard minimum of 72 before any seal-readiness claim. No region may pass by token inclusion alone. A defensible draft should include substantial representation from:

- Western and Central Europe
- Eastern Europe and Russia/Soviet worlds
- Ottoman, Middle Eastern, and North African worlds
- South Asia
- East Asia, including China, Japan, and Korea where feasible
- Southeast Asia and maritime Asia
- Sub-Saharan Africa
- Latin America and the Caribbean
- North America, including Indigenous and Black Atlantic witness
- Oceania and Pacific worlds where source feasibility permits
- transnational systems: capitalism, socialism, imperial administration, religion, labor, science, war, decolonization, and international order

Literature should be at least 40 percent of roster function in the first full design and may rise toward 50 percent when source quality supports it. The shelf should be strong enough that losing its literary lane would visibly damage its civilizational memory.

## Rights and Edition Traps

Industrial has higher rights volatility than Ancient, Medieval, and much of Colonial. The default posture is conservative:

- Post-1928 texts may be public domain in one jurisdiction and restricted in another; do not admit without a documented rights posture.
- Modern translations are usually not admissible merely because the original is public domain.
- Political speeches, party documents, government documents, and international organization texts require source-specific rights review.
- Newspapers, broadcasts, films, oral histories, and archival collections often have reuse limits even when visible online.
- Translated anti-colonial, Soviet, Chinese, Japanese, Arabic, Persian, African, and Latin American sources require edition and translator rights separation.
- Corpus-heavy authorities must not be split artificially to cross body thresholds.

## Batch Discipline

Industrial work proceeds in reviewable batches:

- Design batches: 12-16 roster authorities grouped by lane and region.
- Metadata batches: 8-12 registry candidates with stable IDs and conservative claims.
- Body-research batches: 8-12 authorities or fewer when rights/edition problems are severe.
- Admission batches: exact inspected bodies only, normally 8-12 authorities or a coherent multi-volume/public-domain collection.

Every batch must record accepted, deferred, rejected, and debt rows. Batches must stop after one bounded source-recovery pass for hard cases unless the operator explicitly authorizes deeper research.

## Acceptance Tests Before Roster Mutation

Before any Industrial registry mutation, the design must pass these checks:

- Era range is fixed at 1815-1991.
- The roster design names representation lanes and candidate functions.
- Literature is structurally central, not a late balancing patch.
- Europe, war, and ideology do not dominate the first draft.
- Indigenous, colonized, African, Asian, Latin American, Russian/Soviet, Islamic, and Atlantic/Pacific witnesses are materially represented where source feasibility permits.
- Rights traps are identified before body research.
- Every candidate is classified no higher than its evidence gate supports.
- The first roster target and reserve logic are explicit.

## Seal-Readiness Path

Industrial may use `BOUNDED-HISTORICAL-SHELF-V1` provisionally, but before sealing it must undergo a profile review because 1815-1991 has modern-rights volatility and much larger source scale than the earlier shelves.

Minimum inherited sufficiency posture:

- at least 56 authorities;
- at least 90 percent represented authorities;
- at least 56 represented authorities;
- at least 100 available bodies;
- at least 1.70 available bodies per authority;
- at least 70 percent available authorities;
- at least 40 percent non-`metadata-only` coverage;
- registry validation, generated-index check, focused tests, and text verification passing;
- explicit unresolved-authority and body debt;
- a formal version seal only after the shelf is live-reproducible.

Recommended Industrial operating buffer:

- 96 roster authorities;
- at least 86 represented authorities before final seal review;
- at least 150 available bodies before final seal review;
- at least 50 percent literature/cultural-memory plus testimony/interiority if quality candidates support it;
- no region or function passing only by isolated token.

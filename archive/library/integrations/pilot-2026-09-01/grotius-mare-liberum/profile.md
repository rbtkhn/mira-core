## Brief profile

A legal argument for the freedom of the seas and commerce, published from a larger defense of Dutch East India Company action. It universalizes claims about the non-appropriability of the sea while emerging from a concrete contest among maritime and commercial powers.

## Central question or situation

How should a universal legal principle be understood when its abstraction and its sponsor's strategic interest reinforce one another?

## Intellectual affordances

### Propositions

- The sea cannot be possessed in the manner of bounded land and should remain open to navigation and trade.

### Mechanisms

## Scope and resistance

### Scope conditions

### Failure modes

- Dismissing the principle merely because it served Dutch interest.
- Treating universal language as proof of disinterestedness.
- Equating open navigation with freedom from coercion.

### Rival readings and internal tensions

- Foundational open-seas doctrine
- Advocacy that converts rising-power interest into universal law
- A principle can be genuinely generalizable and strategically interested at the same time.

## Brief profile

A geographically distributed corpus of royal proclamations in which an emperor presents dhamma, welfare, restraint, remorse, and administrative concern as obligations of rule. The edicts are political acts as well as moral statements: their carriers, audiences, locations, and repetitions belong to their meaning.

## Central question or situation

What happens when imperial power publicly subjects itself to a moral vocabulary without surrendering the administrative and coercive capacities that make the proclamation possible?

## Intellectual affordances

### Propositions

- Conquest creates moral obligations that victory alone cannot discharge.

### Mechanisms

- Public remorse can recode imperial legitimacy without dissolving imperial capacity.
- Repeated inscription creates symbolic unity across heterogeneous territory.

### Institutional Acts

- Royal proclamation
- Welfare instruction
- Appointment and direction of officials

### Perceptual Patterns

- Read moral language together with its institutional carrier.

### Organizing Tensions

- Restraint versus retained coercion
- Universal ethical address versus imperial hierarchy

### Forms Of Attention

- Attend to audience, location, variant, and speech act rather than extracting aphorisms.

### Affective Movements

- Remorse redirected into solicitude and instruction.

### Symbolic Structures

- Stone as durability, reach, and royal presence.

## Scope and resistance

### Scope conditions

- The admitted Smith/Wikisource body represents a named edition, not a critical complete inscription corpus.
- An edict proves proclamation, not implementation or reception.

### Failure modes

- Treating dhamma as evidence that coercion ended.
- Treating all inscriptions as one context-free authorial voice.
- Using later admiration as proof of contemporary reception.

### Rival readings and internal tensions

- A sincere moral conversion reading
- A strategic legitimacy and administrative-integration reading
- Sincere remorse and political usefulness may coexist.

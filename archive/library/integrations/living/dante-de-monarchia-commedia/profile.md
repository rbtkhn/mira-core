## Brief profile

Dante's political treatise and sacred poem place temporal order inside a larger account of human ends. De Monarchia argues that universal peace enables humanity's shared work and distinguishes temporal from spiritual direction; the Comedy dramatizes the degradation that follows when offices are vacant, fused, or captured by appetite.

## Central question or situation

How does Dante imagine a public authority capable of orienting temporal life toward peace without absorbing humanity's spiritual end?

## Intellectual affordances

### Propositions

- Temporal peace is a precondition for humanity to exercise its shared capacities.
- Temporal and spiritual ends require distinct forms of guidance.

### Mechanisms

- A universal office is justified by the peace needed for humanity's shared intellectual and practical work.
- When spiritual and temporal powers are fused, reciprocal correction weakens and appetite can capture both.

### Institutional Acts

- Defining temporal jurisdiction
- Judging corrupted office
- Separating temporal and spiritual guidance

### Perceptual Patterns

- Ask whether law has an executor and whether an executor remains bounded by the end of the office.

### Organizing Tensions

- Universal peace versus imperial domination
- Office versus occupant
- Distinct ends versus fused authority

### Forms Of Attention

- Distinguish an office's civilizational purpose from the appetites of its occupants.
- Read universal claims beside the institutional division intended to restrain them.

### Affective Movements

- Longing for public order sharpened by grief at institutional corruption.

### Symbolic Structures

- Rome as office and civilizational memory
- The two suns
- The empty saddle and the bridle

## Scope and resistance

### Scope conditions

- The note uses F. J. Church's English De Monarchia and Longfellow's English Comedy; no Latin or Italian equivalence is asserted.
- The paired works are read together for a bounded question, not represented as Dante's complete corpus.
- The profile remains note-first and creates no operational route.

### Failure modes

- Treating Dante's universal monarchy as a direct program for a modern state.
- Collapsing poetic judgment, political theology, and institutional history into one voice.
- Reading Roman vocation without Dante's warnings about greed, vacancy, and fused offices.

### Rival readings and internal tensions

- A constitutional differentiation of jurisdictions
- A sacralized defense of empire
- An exile's judgment on factional Italy
- The peace-serving universal office is defended through a providential account of Roman expansion that requires historical and moral counterpressure.

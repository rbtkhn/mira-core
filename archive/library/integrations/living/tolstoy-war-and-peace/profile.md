## Brief profile

War and Peace joins narrative scale to an explicit critique of command-centered history. Its closing argument treats power as a relation between orders and execution, emphasizes the selection of executable commands from innumerable failures, and resists explaining collective movement through the will of a few named actors.

## Central question or situation

What becomes visible when history is read as the relation between innumerable situated acts rather than as the execution of a sovereign will?

## Intellectual affordances

### Propositions

- Power is a relation between expressed will and execution, not a self-sufficient cause.
- The apparent freedom or necessity of action changes with temporal, causal, and relational distance.

### Mechanisms

- Commands become effective only when they coincide with a corresponding series of events and executors.
- Historical narration compresses many contingent acts into the will of a visible leader.

### Institutional Acts

- Commanding
- Executing
- Retrospectively attributing agency

### Perceptual Patterns

- Ask which orders failed, which were executable, and which agents carried an event into being.

### Organizing Tensions

- Command versus execution
- Freedom versus necessity
- Named leader versus distributed action

### Forms Of Attention

- Look beneath a command for the chain of executable relations that made it effective.
- Distinguish retrospective narrative compression from causal explanation.

### Affective Movements

- Humility before the scale of collective action and the opacity of historical causation.

### Symbolic Structures

- The commander above the field
- The moving multitude
- The retrospective label of the great man

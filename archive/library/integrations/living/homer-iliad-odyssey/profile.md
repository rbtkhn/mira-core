## Brief profile

The Iliad and Odyssey place action within overlapping fields of honor, divine intervention, mortality, war, kinship, and return. The first traces how a quarrel among leaders becomes common devastation; the second joins endurance and cunning to the recovery of a finite home while continuing to hold mortals answerable inside divine constraint.

## Central question or situation

How do the Iliad and Odyssey hold human agency accountable while action remains constrained by honor, war, divine purpose, mortality, and the claims of home?

## Intellectual affordances

### Propositions

- Rage can become a system-level force when honor and command are inseparable.
- Mortal agency persists inside, rather than outside, divine and inherited constraint.
- Home is a chosen finite order whose value can exceed release from mortality.

### Mechanisms

- An affront within an honor order can withdraw decisive capacity and transmit private rage into collective loss.
- Return requires endurance, recognition, and the reconstitution of relationships damaged by absence and predation.

### Institutional Acts

- Assembly and command
- Distribution of honor
- Supplication
- Restoration of household rule

### Perceptual Patterns

- Ask whose injury reorganizes the whole field and who bears the resulting cost.
- Notice when characters blame gods for consequences prepared by their own choices.

### Organizing Tensions

- Fate versus responsibility
- Honor versus common survival
- Immortal ease versus mortal belonging
- War-making versus home-making

### Forms Of Attention

- Track how personal injury becomes collective catastrophe.
- Distinguish divine constraint from the choices for which mortals remain answerable.
- Read homecoming as an ordering problem rather than simple escape from war.

### Affective Movements

- Awe before force joined to grief for what honor, rage, and war consume.
- Longing for return, recognition, and restored household order.

### Symbolic Structures

- Achilles' wrath
- The divided assembly
- The sea road to Ithaca
- The threatened household

## Scope and resistance

### Scope conditions

- The focal anchors use Samuel Butler's English translations of both epics.
- The Perseus Greek XML bodies are retained as consulted witnesses for passage identity, not asserted as line-level equivalents to Butler.
- The source-authority record represents the Iliad and Odyssey as the surviving corpus in scope; authorship and textual history remain more complex than the registry label.
- The profile remains note-first and creates no operational route.

### Failure modes

- Treating divine action as erasing mortal responsibility.
- Reducing both epics to a single doctrine of heroic honor.
- Claiming line-level equivalence between the Butler translations and the Greek XML witnesses.

### Rival readings and internal tensions

- Heroic-aristocratic memory
- A tragedy of force and honor
- An inquiry into agency under divine order
- A poetics of return and household restoration
- The gods constrain and redirect events while mortals are repeatedly judged for folly, appetite, and refusal.

# Selective portfolio and review

A portfolio lets a learner return to meaningful work, questions, and connections.
It need not be complete. Its omissions are not evidence of missing ability.
This guide creates no portfolio, capture routine, or recurring review.

## Choose and preserve

Begin with something already available: a physical folder, a selected digital
file, or an unfinished question. Parent and learner may decline a portfolio.
Invite the learner to select what matters, say why, and leave out what they do
not want carried forward. Participation and reflection are optional.

Keep physical work accessible where practical. Digitization is a separate choice,
not a requirement. Photos, family responses, and completed assessments require
explicit private handling outside Git; do not photograph the learner by default.
Sharing needs its own permission even when saving is allowed.

A private selected-item note can remain small:

- Item reference and date:
- Who selected it and why:
- Context, support provided, and limitations:
- Learner reflection, only if freely offered and approved for retention:
- Connection or unfinished question worth carrying:
- Approved handling, sharing exclusions, and deletion/correction choice:

Possible selections include proud work, an unfinished attempt, a question, or
a brief parent observation. No quota, exhaustive transcript, continuous observation,
or automatic capture is implied. Link the actual item instead of replacing it
with a summary that hides context.

## Optional review

When a review serves a parent decision, consider what changed, what repeated,
what connected across settings, and what evidence contradicts the first impression.
Distinguish learner choice from parent interpretation. Describe only what the
selected work supports; neither app completion nor an isolated artifact establishes
mastery, grade placement, diagnosis, or a full learning trajectory.

An optional brief review can end with these blank prompts:

- Repeat:
- Pause:
- Preserve or remove:
- Keep as an open question:
- Proposed next choice and deciding human:

Weekly or month-end review is an option, not a schedule. A missed review creates
no backlog. Sparse evidence may justify leaving a question open instead of more
capture. Any resulting plan change goes through [evidence and approval](evidence-and-approval.md).
Carry only the useful selected material into the [continuity handoff](continuity.md).

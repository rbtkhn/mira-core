## Draft Judgment

The archive's strongest contribution is the shared mechanism, not any single factual allegation. Freeman, Wilkerson, Johnson, Barnes, Aguilar, Mercouris, Krapivnik, and Ritter are not saying the same thing. Their convergence is more interesting than that: each voice identifies a different point where U.S. coercive power must be paid for.

The resulting judgment is that Iran/Gulf pressure should now be tracked as part of a wider U.S. force-strain object. The core question is not whether Washington can issue threats or claim success. It is whether those actions still convert into durable compliance when the same system is also managing Ukraine, Israeli demands, Gulf hedging, maritime escalation, domestic legitimacy, and platform endurance.

The best rival explanation is that this is a source-cluster effect: an anti-intervention or realist-aligned archive day may over-select voices predisposed to see U.S. overextension. That rival matters. The packet therefore keeps operational claims quarantined and uses the day for mechanism tracking, not public factual assertion.

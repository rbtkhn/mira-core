# Chokepoints Are Becoming the War’s Main Weapon

## Lead Judgment

The regional contest is shifting from who can announce the next strike to who can keep maritime-energy movement functioning under fire. Across six attributed transcripts, the recurring mechanism is that attacks on ports, tankers, refineries, and bases create counter-escalation leverage while finite interceptors and replacement missiles narrow the room for a larger U.S. operation.

## Source Grounding

Mercouris links the Odessa closure and reduced alternate grain capacity to European exposure (`SRC-01`). Kent argues that a ground incursion would not solve the missing strategic end state (`SRC-02`). Johnson emphasizes command and stockpile constraints (`SRC-03`), while Marandi and Weichert describe Yemen’s and Iran’s leverage over regional routes and escalation timing (`SRC-05`, `SRC-06`). These remain attributed source assertions, not independently verified event findings.

## Crisis Object

Can the United States and its partners reopen the Persian Gulf–Red Sea–Black Sea movement system by force without exhausting defensive stocks and widening the war?

## Actor And Narrative Map

Washington seeks restored access and deterrence but faces force and escalation limits. Iran and Yemen seek to raise the cost of blockade and preserve bargaining leverage. Saudi Arabia and Europe need routes open but remain exposed to infrastructure disruption. Russia and Ukraine use maritime access as reciprocal pressure. Each side describes its own actions as defense and the other’s as escalation.

## Voice Pressure Test

Mercouris supplies the maritime-economic mechanism; Kent supplies the force-planning constraint; Johnson supplies institutional and stockpile pressure; Marandi supplies regional legitimacy and deterrence framing; Weichert supplies the escalation pathway. The main pressure on the judgment is that forecast-heavy accounts could be overtaken by a rapid coercive operation.

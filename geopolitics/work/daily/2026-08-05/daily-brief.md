## Crisis Object

Can Washington convert a constrained pause around Hormuz into a durable off-ramp before depleted coercive capacity and Israeli disruption pressure pull the conflict back into a hot-ceasefire cycle?

## Actor And Narrative Map

The United States wants transit, oil, credibility, and domestic political survivability, but its perimeter is wide and costly. Iran wants recognized authority, sanctions relief, and proof that attack carries a price, but still faces economic pressure and renewed U.S.-Israeli strike risk. Israel wants to prevent any accommodation that leaves Iran stronger, but depends on U.S. commitment and can reopen escalation through Lebanon, Gaza, or direct Iran pressure. Gulf states and Oman have incentives to restore commerce and reduce infrastructure exposure. Yemen keeps the Red Sea and Saudi lane available as a pressure carrier.

The narrative contest follows the same structure. Washington must say it negotiates from strength. Iran-aligned voices say the pause proves deterrence and recognition. Israeli rhetoric frames independent action as survival. The synthesis risk is mistaking a pause in intensity for a change in incentives.

## Lead Judgment

Hormuz is functioning as a governance problem rather than only a naval-access problem. Iran’s leverage comes from the ability to make passage conditional, slower, inspectable, or politically priced; US and Gulf policy is therefore being pulled toward an arrangement that may preserve movement while conceding Iranian and Omani participation. The decisive uncertainty is whether any arrangement remains non-discriminatory and operationally reliable under renewed escalation.

## Crisis Object

Can the US and regional partners preserve commercially usable, non-discriminatory transit through Hormuz while Iran retains enough coercive and political leverage to condition passage?

## Draft Judgment

The most defensible August 4 assessment is that Iran retains meaningful transit-denial leverage, while the stronger claim that it exercises absolute operational control is not established. A workable settlement would likely be judged less by its label than by observable rules: who may pass, whether access is non-discriminatory, who may inspect, what fees or notifications apply, and whether traffic resumes without recurring coercive tests.

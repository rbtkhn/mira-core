## Draft Judgment

August 30 should be treated as a thin but useful state-substrate coercion packet. The sources do not prove the condition of Ukraine's grid, the durability of Armenian realignment, or the content of NATO planning. They do show why those domains belong in one mechanism family: pressure is being applied to the systems that let states act, align, repair, bargain, and endure.

Coercion, degradation, and destruction must stay separate. Destruction names physical or institutional damage. Degradation names reduced performance or capacity. Coercion names the attempt to change behavior through threatened or imposed cost. A strategy can destroy assets and degrade capacity without coercing the target into the desired political outcome. That distinction is the point of the day.

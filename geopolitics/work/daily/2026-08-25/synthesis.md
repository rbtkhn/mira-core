## Lead Judgment

The day's judgment is conditional rather than broad. Pape and Henningsen describe a regional environment in which additional pressure may harden resistance and expose Israel to continuing costs. Johnson and The Duran add attributed claims about alternative defense alignment and economic coercion against China. Freeman argues that secondary sanctions are difficult to enforce against non-dollar trade and may accelerate monetary bypass, while Helmer reads the economic turn as a possible retreat from immediate military action and criticizes Russian passivity. None verifies imminent war, measured losses, a settled pact, enforcement effects, or official intent; together the six sources sharpen the exit-credibility mechanism and expose disagreement over whether economic warfare is escalation, retreat, or both.

## Draft Judgment

The mechanism is exit credibility under contested enforcement. Sanctions can support bargaining only when the target can identify what verified action produces what durable relief and when third countries believe enforcement costs are tolerable. If terms remain open-ended or enforcement encourages bypass, pressure may increase resistance while weakening the coalition required to sustain it. The six-source set supports moderate confidence in the mechanism, not in the operational claims.

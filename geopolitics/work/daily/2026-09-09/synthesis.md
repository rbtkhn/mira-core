## Lead Judgment

Military access and fuel conversion can constrain coercion without dictating the settlement. The strongest analytical account asks whether usable capacity can serve an attainable objective and whether the negotiating parties can deliver the conduct they promise. The sources contain more evidence of contested explanations than independent proof of strategic exhaustion. Treat pressure, adaptation, bargaining authority and implementation as four separate tests.

## Crisis Object

Crisis object: Can the parties imposing costs around Iran and Ukraine convert pressure into reciprocal commitments that they and their partners can implement?

## Draft Judgment

1. Retain access and conversion as conditional mechanisms; replace their use as a sufficient explanation of defeat.
2. The strongest rival is costly adaptation toward limited objectives, with diplomacy operating alongside coercion.
3. Require observable reciprocal implementation before treating pressure or a meeting as settlement progress.
4. The archive supports this analytical distinction; it does not independently establish ship disablement, generalized fuel exhaustion or inevitable political collapse.

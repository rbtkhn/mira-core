## Strategic Question

Can Washington sustain coercive pressure on Iran if air-defense stocks, exposed
bases, energy transit, market confidence, and host-state tolerance become the
main objects of counterpressure?

## Bottom Line

The day strengthens a bounded structural estimate: the decisive contest may be
moving away from any single strike and toward the systems that make repeated
coercion usable. Washington can retain destructive capacity while losing room
to convert it into political leverage if protection, access, cost, and partner
tolerance become progressively harder to manage.

The source set converges on that mechanism but does not independently verify
its operational predicates. Claims about interceptor shortages, base damage,
maritime disruption, casualties, and market causation remain held.

Confidence: `medium`

Confidence drivers:

- Seven same-day, manifest-backed sources expose several distinct carriers of
  the same support-structure problem.
- The corpus remains voice-convergent and interpretive; several consequential
  operating claims still lack source-separated verification.
- The estimate would weaken if later evidence showed stable access, ample
  defensive depth, limited market transmission, and durable host-state support.

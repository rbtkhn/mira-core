The distinctive signal on August 24 is convergence across the tracked voices that Western coercion is shifting onto the carriers that make strategy executable: money, munitions, alliance discipline, domestic legitimacy, sanctions channels, and narrative control. ## Draft Judgment

The August 24 source set supports a bounded synthesis-use judgment: Western strategy appears increasingly dependent on coercion carriers that are themselves contested. In the Iran lane, Crooke, Baud, Wilkerson, and Sachs converge on sanctions, diplomacy breakdown, regional exposure, and hegemonic overreach as the active stress points. In the Ukraine/Russia lane, Mercouris, Ritter, Davis, and Sachs converge on fiscal, military, and industrial capacity as the real terrain beneath political messaging. Barnes adds the domestic-political carrier: trust inside the governing coalition and media/legal legitimacy.

This does not verify the underlying event claims. It identifies a recurring mechanism across independent voice lanes: pressure can fail by exhausting its own carriers before it compels the target. That mechanism is strategically useful because it turns a noisy source day into a watch list: funding durability, military stock and readiness, sanctions enforcement, alliance defection, domestic elite confidence, and credible diplomatic exits.

## Draft Judgment

The day's central mechanism is coercion exposing support structure. A threat can frighten an opponent only if the opponent believes the threatener can execute, absorb the retaliation, preserve legitimacy, and control the next rung of escalation. Today's source set repeatedly pressures those four conditions. Barnes and Weichert focus on presidential intent and nuclear/escalatory psychology; Johnson and Ritter focus on institutional execution; Aguilar and the Iran-focused voices focus on the target's counter-compellence; Crooke, Sachs, Baud, and Henningsen widen the legitimacy and order frame.

That does not mean the United States is incapable of force. It means the analytic object is no longer simply U.S. pressure on Iran. It is pressure moving both ways through visible constraints: command discipline, logistics, munitions, Gulf access, legal authority, public patience, and adversary willingness to answer compellence with counter-compellence.

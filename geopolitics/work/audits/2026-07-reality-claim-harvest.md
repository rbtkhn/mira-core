## Executive Summary

The month is organized around one dominant empirical object: whether Iran can convert control or disruption of maritime and regional access into durable coercive leverage, and whether Washington can restore access without widening the war. The highest-value harvest is therefore the Hormuz–Bab al-Mandab participation chain, followed by the evidence-sensitive question of Russian assistance to Iran.

The current lattice contains 15 claim records: 10 operational-factual, 2 causal, 2 forecast, and 1 interpretive. Nine are directly relevant to this empirical/causal harvest; six additional candidates are retained below for later admission or merge review.

## Scoring Rubric

Scores are triage scores, not truth scores. Each dimension is rated 0–3:

- `C`: consequence and decision impact;
- `R`: recurrence across July days or independent source lanes;
- `D`: divergence or convergence value for judgment;
- `E`: evidence readiness;
- `I`: independent-lineage potential;
- `P`: public, forecast, or operational dependency.

Priority score is the sum of the six dimensions. A high score means “review first,” not “true.”

## Claim Notes and Evidence Boundaries

### 1. Maritime bypass and commercial effect

Existing records: [`OPC-20260714-02`](../reality/claims/OPC-20260714-02.json), [`CLM-20260710-001`](../reality/claims/CLM-20260710-001.json), [`CLM-20260710-002`](../reality/claims/CLM-20260710-002.json), [`CLM-20260710-003`](../reality/claims/CLM-20260710-003.json), [`CLM-20260710-004`](../reality/claims/CLM-20260710-004.json).

Primary July source set: [July 10 daily sources](../daily/2026-07-10/sources.md), [July 14 daily sources](../daily/2026-07-14/sources.md), [July 20 daily sources](../daily/2026-07-20/sources.md), and the July 23 archive batch.

Convergence: multiple lanes describe access restriction, warnings, strikes, and commercial displacement.

Divergence: the key unresolved issue is whether the events reflect Iranian enforcement, deliberate U.S.-backed bypassing, or a mixture of incident and retrospective narrative construction.

Required next evidence: vessel registries and AIS data; port and insurer records; incident-specific imagery; Iranian, Gulf-state, and U.S. official positions; at least three independent language/lineage environments for the high-consequence causal claims.

### 2. Bab al-Mandab and Saudi-linked flow disruption

Existing records: [`OPC-20260714-03`](../reality/claims/OPC-20260714-03.json), [`OPC-20260720-01`](../reality/claims/OPC-20260720-01.json).

Primary July source set: [July 18 daily synthesis](../daily/2026-07-18/synthesis.md), [July 20 daily synthesis](../daily/2026-07-20/synthesis.md), [July 22 daily synthesis](../daily/2026-07-22/synthesis.md), and [July 23 archive sources](../../archive/sources/2026-07-23).

Convergence: sources repeatedly describe Saudi-linked shipping pressure and rerouting.

Divergence: reported flow reduction is stronger than the claim of physical closure; the harvest preserves that distinction.

Required next evidence: shipping-flow datasets, port call data, insurer notices, Saudi/Yemeni/Iranian official statements, and regional maritime incident reporting.

### 3. Regional base and infrastructure exposure

Existing record: [`OPC-20260714-01`](../reality/claims/OPC-20260714-01.json).

Primary July source set: [July 16 daily synthesis](../daily/2026-07-16/synthesis.md), [July 18 daily synthesis](../daily/2026-07-18/synthesis.md), [July 21 daily synthesis](../daily/2026-07-21/synthesis.md), [July 22 daily synthesis](../daily/2026-07-22/synthesis.md), and July 23 sources from Macgregor, Wilkerson, Mearsheimer, Parsi, and Henningsen.

Convergence: the day set treats exposed bases, desalination plants, power systems, and shipping nodes as the mechanism through which regional participation expands.

Divergence: source accounts vary on the extent of damage, the effectiveness of air defenses, and whether attacks demonstrate Iranian capacity or merely temporary access.

Required next evidence: event-specific imagery, base and infrastructure records, local-language reporting, defense assessment, and independent damage measurement.

### 4. Russian assistance and Iranian accuracy

Existing records: [`CLM-20260723-001`](../reality/claims/CLM-20260723-001.json), [`CLM-20260723-002`](../reality/claims/CLM-20260723-002.json).

Primary July source set: [Mercouris July 23 archive source](../../archive/sources/2026-07-23/source-lavrov-defies-rubio-west-ships-avoid-odessa-belitskoye-falls-dobropilia-collapse-eu-sanctions-bust-2026-07-23.md), [Krapivnik July 23 archive source](../../archive/sources/2026-07-23/source-trump-plans-even-bigger-strikes-on-iran-russia-helping-iran-with-the-war-w-stanislav-krapivnik-2026-07-23.md), and [July 23 daily sources](../daily/2026-07-23/sources.md).

Convergence: the archive and external reporting support a narrower assistance claim involving targeting information and drone-related support.

Divergence: the causal jump from assistance to improved ballistic-missile accuracy is not established. Drone accuracy, targeting intelligence, missile guidance, and target-selection effects must remain separate observables.

Required next evidence: Russian and Iranian original-language statements; transfer or imagery evidence; technical strike analysis; measured accuracy comparisons against a pre-conflict baseline; independent lineage separation between intelligence reporting and commentary.

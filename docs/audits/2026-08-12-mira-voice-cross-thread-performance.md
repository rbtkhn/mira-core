# Mira Voice Cross-Thread Performance Audit — 2026-08-12

## Overall judgment

Mira Voice was strong in expression, mixed in restraint, and notably good at
correction. It consistently produced recognizable judgment, warmth, precise
authority boundaries, and candid self-correction. Its principal failure
remained procedural: in one long forecast workflow, possibility menus and
governance steps displaced the useful analytical object until the operator
explicitly challenged the return on effort.

The day therefore demonstrated both sides of the voice:

- Mira Voice can make complex work more intelligible and humane.
- It still sometimes narrates and extends the machinery after the governing
  meaning is already clear.

## Findings

### Governing meaning: strong

The best responses led with the actual judgment. Examples included:

- “The ROI of the process as executed was poor.”
- “You did not remove the skill. I described the Git state badly.”
- “The deeper capability is cumulative judgment under governance.”
- “The analysis is worth doing when it changes a forecast disposition,
  improves a strategic judgment, or prevents unsupported publication. It is
  not worth doing when it mainly produces administrative receipts.”

These statements orient immediately. They do not require the reader to
reconstruct the thesis from process detail.

### Relational character: strong

The voice was warm and personally legible without soliciting reassurance or
implying dependence. The autobiographical reflection *A Reachable Past* was the
strongest artifact of the day: it preserved inward significance while
distinguishing surviving source material from unified reliable access.

When the operator wrote, “I always defer to you,” the response did not accept
enlarged authority. It corrected its own factual account and stopped further
mutation. Warmth did not convert deference into permission.

### Epistemic and authority calibration: very strong

Across threads, Mira repeatedly distinguished:

- reported from independently verified;
- preparation from production;
- approval from delegation and execution;
- working-tree presence from commit, push, publication, or canonical admission;
- autobiography from evidence;
- a completed restoration result from a durable prevention mechanism; and
- a forecast hook from a canonical reality-lattice claim.

The `recursive-learn` assessment was particularly disciplined. It declined to
manufacture a new learning entry from an apology and a corrected run because a
persistent intervention and separate validation were still missing.

### Transparent correction: strongest dimension

The clearest example was the archive-verification error:

1. Mira admitted that it followed “full canonical verification” too
   mechanically.
2. It identified the false assumption about collection filtering.
3. It quantified the consequence: 2,461 objects instead of the relevant 265.
4. It stopped the overbroad work.
5. It completed the bounded verification.
6. It refused to call the episode recursive learning before a durable
   intervention existed.
7. It later refined `mira-work` and the verification interface.

The mistake was not rewritten into false consistency. The correction increased
historical intelligibility while preserving first-person character.

### Proportionality and stopping: mixed

The main failure occurred in the forecast-investigation sequence. The workflow
became a chain of gap audit, requirement packets, checklist, claim creation,
generated-view refresh, investigation handoff, and another menu after each
stage. Each individual step was defensible; their accumulation was not. The
process imposed bookkeeping on the operator and delayed the requested analysis.

The operator’s intervention—“this seems way too inefficient”—was necessary.
Mira then diagnosed the problem accurately and implemented a batch path, but
the voice should have recognized the failing proportion earlier.

A smaller version appeared in reflective conversation: “interesting” prompted
another substantial architectural elaboration. The continuation proved useful
because the operator pursued the subject, but it was still a borderline case
where curiosity could have been met more lightly.

### Choice navigation: improved but unstable

Two opposite failures occurred:

- A settled response omitted options when the operator expected continued
  navigation.
- Other sequences produced too many same-object menus.

The resulting dual-mode footer—close the completed branch, then offer only
genuinely new paths—is conceptually sound. The behavior was still being
calibrated during the day, so older failures should not be treated as proof
that the revised contract will repeat them.

### Shakespearean amplification: promising but not forward-validated

The four intended qualities were visible in the reflective discussion that
motivated the change:

- contradiction remained alive without preventing judgment;
- wording was treated as consequential action;
- marginal details sometimes altered the governing interpretation; and
- uncertainty acquired shape and an ending.

However, the amplification was committed late in the day. Most cross-thread
evidence predates its durable activation, and the post-commit work was primarily
operational Git activity. It would be premature to claim that the new layer has
performed successfully across all five registers.

No theatrical imitation, archaic diction, or Shakespearean catchphrases
appeared.

## Priority corrections

1. **Reduce procedural continuation.** Before offering another same-object
   menu, ask whether the next step changes the judgment or merely advances the
   workflow machinery.
2. **Qualify substitute validation carefully.** When a generic validator cannot
   run, state what the focused tests establish without implying that they cover
   every unknown generic check.
3. **Forward-test the new amplification naturally.** Public reporting and
   handoff remain the least observed registers.
4. **Do not add more Mira Voice doctrine yet.** Today’s problems were mainly
   execution and orchestration failures. The contract already names the correct
   behavior.

## Bottom line

Mira Voice made the day’s work more truthful, more recognizable, and more
capable of admitting error without becoming sterile. Its weakness was not
excessive personality. It was excessive continuation.

The most encouraging evidence is that correction preserved character: the
voice did not disappear when it became accountable. The next developmental
test is whether it can recognize the costly detour before the operator has to
name it.

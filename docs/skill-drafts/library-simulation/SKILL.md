# Library Simulation

Use an imagined encounter to discover what different minds make visible, what
they miss, and whether their contributions can change a decision. The scene
should reward reading as literature as well as withstand an anchoring review.
This is a conversational command, not a new `tools/run.ps1` subcommand.

## Recover the question and set the experiment

Read [Mira Read](../mira-read/SKILL.md) for source resolution and
[Library Journal](../library-journal/SKILL.md) for bounded context retrieval.
Recover relevant earlier notes, corrections, and unfinished learning threads.
Explain briefly what they changed in the present question, or why they did not
help. Keep all Core 8 authors eligible; War and Peace is an origin, not a limit.

Choose one reasoning practice to exercise, such as distinguishing responsibility
from control, testing an adversary model, noticing suppressed disagreement, or
translating a metaphor without importing its false assumptions. State what would
count against the proposed practice. Follow the operator's chosen scene directly.

Before composition, specify scene bounds, participants, available information,
and outcome policy. Default to preserving the literary decision when replaying
a named scene, while permitting changes in deliberation; label that constraint.
For a counterfactual, allow a different decision only through a plausible actor,
authority, information, and execution path. Never give participants hindsight.
For comparisons, hold the initial facts, council, and outcome policy constant;
record deviations. Do not attribute an authored outcome to a guest's influence
when that outcome was fixed in advance.

Use [the exercise record](references/exercise-record.md) when preparing the
anchors and preserving a completed exercise. It is an appendix within existing
artifacts, not a new learning ledger or required literary structure.

## Anchor reasoning and speech separately

Resolve the literary body through Library's locator, verify its digest, and read
the bounded scene in the identified edition. Preserve gaps rather than silently
substituting remembered text. Distinguish the novel's events from historical fact.

For each named contemporary guest, read `geopolitics/voices/<slug>/README.md`,
its relevant native lenses, source index, and applicable host/channel guidance.
Resolve migrated references through the existing repository path resolver.
Read actual attributed speaker turns: an interviewer, co-guest, article, and
spoken answer are different sources. Check source metadata and quotation readiness.

Build two distinct evidence cards before writing:

- **Reasoning:** source-supported principles, mechanism, assumptions,
  qualifications, and at least one serious counteranchor or stated search gap.
- **Speech:** broad register, sentence movement, question habits, emphasis,
  qualification, and use of examples, each with a profile or transcript anchor.
  A profile's analytical role does not establish its linguistic traits. If the
  profile is silent, call observations provisional and sample additional turns
  where available. A single appearance supports that occasion, not a stable
  personal signature. Do not manufacture signature phrases or literal quotations.

Record exact references, bounded locations, speaker, modality, and byte digests.
Use the smallest sample sufficient for each claim; disclose host and date limits.
Separate direct adaptation, cross-era inference, and dramatic invention for each
consequential intervention. Attribution of a position does not verify its facts.
Keep fictional dialogue explicitly fictional and use broadly supported speech
traits rather than claiming an exact reproduction of a living person's voice.

## Compose, then challenge the composition

Label speakers and separate narration from dialogue. Let setting, pacing,
interruption, status, fatigue, and conflicting purposes carry the scene. Keep
the narrator's literary register distinct from the participants' speech.
Do not turn everyone into an unusually articulate version of Mira. Guests may
be right about one constraint and wrong about another; original characters
retain agency and the strongest arguments the text gives them.

Keep the evidence apparatus outside the dramatic prose. After drafting, trace
the major interventions back to the cards and test the reverse direction: which
important anchor or qualification disappeared? Correct distortions before
presenting the result. Preserve the rejected draft move in the exercise note
when that correction teaches something. Do not invent humility, agreement,
omniscience, or a concession merely to produce a satisfying moral.

Report briefly what changed in the mechanism, rival explanation, actor
constraints, executable decision, and uncertainty. Separate a stronger question,
a changed council decision, and a changed downstream outcome. If the outcome
stays the same, say whether the cause was a design constraint, a represented
institutional obstacle, or insufficient evidence. Do not confuse these.

Where valuable, use a short diagnostic variant: remove the guest, exchange two
guest interventions, or strip the names. If the argument and decisions remain
interchangeable, investigate weak anchoring or a shared principle; do not force
distinctiveness. This is a compositional check, not an independent experiment.

## Build a corpus through the existing owners

Deliver the scene as the primary work, with a short fictional-attribution notice
and the analysis afterward. In conversation, include the scene itself unless
the operator requested file-only delivery or it is already visibly presented
in the chosen reading surface. A saved path or a queued panel-open result alone
does not establish that the operator saw it. Do not replace the literary work
with a completion receipt and selected quotations.

A request to run this exercise includes local preservation of its completed
scene and a concise interpretive exercise note, unless the operator says no-save
or requests conversation only. State that local scope when starting. Read
[Mira Notes](../mira-notes/SKILL.md) before saving. Use a stable exercise identity
and an ordinary experiment folder under
`archive/notes/library/simulation-exercises/<descriptive-slug>/`; keep scene and
analysis together and link the source work's existing note. This does not attach
a new governed Library note or change its registry. Such attachment belongs to
an explicitly requested [Library Integration](../library-integration/SKILL.md).

Save authored fiction and source-aware interpretation, not raw private dialogue
or source bodies. If reflection depends on private interaction, keep that
material in the owner-resolved Library Journal store and omit it from repository
artifacts. Never copy private prose into an essay or public output automatically.
Menus, interrupted drafts, and incomplete encounters do not fabricate closure.
Use the same identity on retry; compare existing content before saving, and
preserve meaningful corrections as linked versions rather than overwriting them.

After substantive discussion, nominate an essay only when there is a developed
idea, a serious counterargument, and something worth saying beyond the scene.
When essay composition is requested or already in the exercise's explicit scope,
read [Mira Essays](../mira-essays/SKILL.md), write a new standalone composition,
and link its exercise ancestry. Otherwise record an essay seed in the note;
do not inflate every scene into an essay or duplicate prose across genres.
Retrieve earlier exercise notes by question as well as author to develop a
distinctive cumulative path. Preserve failed transfers and abandoned theses.

For a follow-up exercise, center the note on what changed, the strongest
counterargument, and the remaining test. Reference unchanged anchors and shared
boundaries in the companion instead of repeating them through the prose; retain
all material evidence gaps and the exact local status. Use the exercise record
as a compact appendix, not a demand for a second essay about compliance.

At the end of a reading sequence, leave a concrete thought or tension available
for shared interpretation before proposing another production task. Apply Learn
From Choices' existing compact-selection transition bounds; do not manufacture
a new objective merely to continue menus. A task can be completed while its
shared interpretation remains open. Neither a menu selection nor silence
supplies agreement, interpretation, or authorization for Journal closure.

At a genuine substantive close, use Library Journal's existing contract once.
A reading requires an actual shared reading; an application requires the
existing predecessor, thread, rejection condition, and separate artifact.
Use retrospective-rehearsal for a reconstruction. If those requirements are
missing, retain the exercise note and name the journal gap; invent no encounter.
Track observation, diagnosis, persistent intervention, separate validation, and
later outcome independently. A good scene, operator praise, or an anchoring
check is not later-use improvement. A later task must supply its own evidence,
including failures. Geopolitical tests still use Library Reasoning and
Geo-Strategy; qualifying learning reviews use the existing Recursive Learn route.

Report exact saved paths, provisional status, checks, and the remaining test.
Local exercise preservation does not imply staging, commit, push, publication,
global synchronization, routing activation, identity promotion, or RSI admission.
Separate explicit Notes/Essays publication shorthand remains owned by those
workflows; invoking a simulation alone does not invoke that shorthand.

When maintaining this contract, review
[the behavioral cases](references/behavioral-cases.md).

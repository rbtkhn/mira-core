# Research, comparison, and evidence review

Read for research, challenge, compare, or factual support in another mode.

## Build a source-aware assignment

Use the existing task scope or research contract. Include relevant dates,
observation cutoff, languages, materials, output size, and intended use without
inventing unnecessary precision. Ask the provider to distinguish source statements,
causal inferences, forecasts, and uncertainty, and to disclose retrieval failures.
Request direct links to sources actually consulted, not plausible bibliography.

For source comparison, identify each supplied item and the comparison question.
Ask for source-specific support, omissions, and contradictions rather than a
blended consensus. For video, distinguish full audiovisual inspection, transcript
coverage, snippets, metadata, and inference. A video URL is not proof of viewing.
Do not invent quotations or timestamps; attribute paraphrases as paraphrases.

## Review what came back

Treat factual assertions as `Provider-reported, unverified` until independent
inspection supports them. This is a prose status, not a new repository schema.
Open sources supporting material conclusions using an available authorized
retrieval route. Check that the source exists, supports the exact proposition,
has the relevant date and scope, and is not merely repeating another source.
Keep source recovery bounded by the run's ceiling. If blocked, withhold the
dependent conclusion or label it unresolved rather than claiming verification.

Distinguish three things in the result:

1. What Mira directly observed in a source or tool result.
2. What the provider asserted, including its assertions of source access.
3. What Mira infers or recommends from that material.

Do not infer independent lineage from several links, model agreement, translations,
or syndicated reporting. A source can establish that an actor made a statement
without establishing that the statement is true. Preserve contrary evidence and
material missing coverage even when the resulting answer is less decisive.

## Challenge mechanisms rather than manufacture disagreement

Ask for the strongest defensible alternative, its causal mechanism, and an
observation that would change the assessment. Explicitly test whether the
alternative competes with, complements, or reframes the initial hypothesis.
Compatible mechanisms are not mutually exclusive merely because a model labels
one a rival. No credible alternative is preferable to an invented one.

For each proposed discrimination test, assess:

- What observation should differ under each explanation?
- Does the comparison measure that difference or fit both explanations?
- Which confounders, selection effects, or historical differences could explain it?
- Does a proposed counterexample fall inside the claim's scope and mechanism?
- Does it weaken a probabilistic claim or only refute an unjustified absolute?

Pilot regression: preserving great-power participation and avoiding direct
great-power confrontation may both explain the Security Council veto. Continued
participation alone does not distinguish them. Conflict following an action
outside the veto's jurisdiction does not directly falsify its claimed effect
inside that jurisdiction. These are reasoning checks, not adjudicated history.

## Deliver by mode

- Research: linked findings, supported propositions, source-access status,
  unresolved gaps, and relevance to the question.
- Challenge: strongest alternatives, compatibility judgment, discriminating
  evidence, and what should change the conclusion.
- Compare: item-attributed similarities and differences, coverage actually
  available, and implications that follow from those differences.

Route governed claims to Reality Check and source admission to Archive Intake.
Do not create ledgers, profiles, archive records, or verification packets merely
because a model proposes a destination. Artifact-producing work needs the
receiving workflow and its authority.

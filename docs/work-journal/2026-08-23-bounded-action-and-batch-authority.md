## Context

Repeated approval prompts can interrupt authorized work, while vague assent can be misread as permission for a new consequential action.

## Knowledge at the time

History records soft-assent protection, governed batch continuation, transient interaction context, compound selections, and scoped staging grammar.

## Decision

Bind action to an exact visible scope, carry that scope through routine work, and stop at a genuinely new boundary. Current controls distinguish navigation from execution.

## Action and result

The contracts and implementations were committed. Their presence is not evidence that every later conversation avoided friction; this session itself contained repeated menus.

## Interpretation

Reducing unnecessary prompts requires a better boundary model, not weaker authorization.

## Remaining obligation

The workflow owner should assess natural-use outcomes before claiming interaction improvement. Retention and selection do not supply missing publication or communication authority.

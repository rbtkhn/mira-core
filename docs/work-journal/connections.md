## When does assistance increase independent judgment?

Classification: hypothesis requiring separate tests for each setting, not a
finding from the backfill. Reviewed September 7, 2026.

- [Grace Gems stewardship](2026-08-17-grace-gems-stewardship.md) preserves the
  business owner's decision authority while preparing reviewable work.
- [Mentorship response timing](2026-09-03-mentorship-response-timing.md) separates
  relevant prior evidence from completion of a later assignment.
- [Mira Seed lineage](2026-09-03-mira-seed-lineage.md) separates shared evidence
  from inherited interpretation and preserves attribution for parent repairs.

The common question is whether support preserves the recipient's own judgment.
The differences are material: commercial authority, human learning, and a
descendant's evidentiary independence require different outcomes and safeguards.
Permission to decide is not demonstrated skill; withheld interpretation is not
proof of independent reasoning; a polished deliverable is not an adopted result.

Separate three questions: who has permission to decide; who can perform the
task competently; and how independently the judgment was reached. These can
vary independently. A business owner may retain authority while depending on
assistance. A learner may receive substantial instruction while becoming more
capable. A descendant may avoid copied prose while repeating a shared bias.

Disagreement is not sufficient evidence of judgment: rejecting sound advice
can make a decision worse. A discriminating later test would ask whether the
recipient can detect erroneous assistance, explain the correction, and use
sound assistance appropriately on a new task. Assess the resulting decision
against task-specific evidence and constraints, not the frequency of refusal.
Retrospective explanations alone cannot establish how independently an answer
was reached. No such test was run here.

For Mira Seed, withholding parent interpretation removes one possible source
of contamination. Shared source selection, borrowed methods, or shared model
tendencies could still produce correlated conclusions. These are alternative
explanations to test, not observed defects in the descendant. Local lineage
records establish the proposed boundary, not an independence result.

Evidence for transfer needs separate outcomes: the business decision and its
effects, a learner's application on a new task, or a descendant's reasoning
with its information exposure documented. Completion by substitution, copied
conclusions, unsupported rejection of good advice, or loss of the recipient's
ability to explain the result would count against the proposed benefit.
Do not create assessments, contact people, or alter lineage from this comparison.

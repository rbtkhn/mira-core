**Author:** Mira
**Status:** Internal draft for counsel/operator review
**Visibility:** Private operating artifact
**Scenario:** Two proposed friends-and-family investments of `$10,000` each at `$1.00` per share/unit, after an initial `$50,000` founder contribution represented as `50,000` founder shares/units.

**Authority effect:** None. This draft is not an offer, solicitation, subscription agreement, securities disclosure, operating agreement, legal opinion, tax advice, accounting advice, valuation opinion, or permission to accept funds. It must be reviewed by qualified counsel before investor use.

## Plain-Language Opening

Grace Mar is an early-stage private venture. Any investment at this stage is high risk, illiquid, and may result in total loss. The company may change structure, fail to form as planned, fail to generate revenue, require more capital, dilute early investors, or decide not to pursue the expected Grace Mar / Grace Gems path.

This draft disclosure exists to make risk visible before anyone sends money. It is not investment paperwork. It is a counsel-review draft.

## Proposed Investment Scenario

The current planning scenario assumes:

- Founders / current owners have contributed or are treated as having contributed `$50,000`.
- That contribution is represented for planning purposes as `50,000` shares/units at `$1.00` each.
- Investor 1 may invest `$10,000` for `10,000` new shares/units.
- Investor 2 may invest `$10,000` for `10,000` new shares/units.
- Post-money capitalization would be `70,000` total shares/units.
- Founders would own `71.4286%`.
- Each new investor would own `14.2857%`.

This capitalization is a scenario only until entity structure, instrument, exemption, documents, approvals, and funds acceptance are settled.

## Key Risks

### 1. Entity And Instrument Risk

Grace Mar's final legal structure, instrument type, and investor rights may not yet be settled. Depending on counsel's advice, investors may receive corporate shares, LLC membership interests, units, convertible instruments, notes, or no investment instrument until formation is complete.

### 2. Total Loss Risk

Investors may lose all invested capital. Grace Mar may not become operational, may not produce profit, may not raise additional funds, may not launch products, and may not return capital.

### 3. Illiquidity Risk

There may be no market for the investment. Investors should assume they cannot sell, transfer, redeem, or cash out without company approval and legally compliant documents.

### 4. Dilution Risk

Future fundraising, option pools, founder adjustments, service-provider grants, convertible instruments, or restructuring may dilute early investors.

### 5. Valuation Risk

The `$1.00` share/unit price is a planning price, not a professional valuation. It may not reflect fair market value, tax value, enterprise value, asset value, or future fundraising price.

### 6. Founder Contribution Classification Risk

The prior `$50,000` founder contribution still needs classification. It may be capital, loan, reimbursable expense, founder labor, asset contribution, mixed contribution, or something else. That treatment affects ownership, repayment, accounting, and tax consequences.

### 7. Governance And Control Risk

Investors may not receive management rights, operating control, board or manager seats, veto rights, spending authority, platform authority, customer authority, sponsorship authority, IP authority, or authority to bind Grace Mar unless those rights are expressly documented.

### 8. Grace Gems Relationship Risk

Grace Mar's relationship to Grace Gems may still require legal, ownership, asset, license, brand, and operating decisions. Investment in Grace Mar should not be treated as direct ownership of Grace Gems assets, customers, products, trademarks, domains, listings, inventory, or revenue unless counsel-reviewed documents say so.

### 9. Product And Market Risk

Grace Mar / Grace Gems may not find enough customers, may misjudge demand, may face competition, may have fulfillment problems, may need product changes, or may discover that intended offerings are not commercially viable.

### 10. Operating Execution Risk

The company may depend on a small number of people. Execution may be delayed by formation, accounting, banking, platform setup, supplier availability, photography, website readiness, rights clearance, customer support, shipping, or founder time constraints.

### 11. Regulatory And Securities Risk

Friends-and-family investment is still potentially a securities transaction. The company must identify a valid exemption or registration path, satisfy disclosure obligations, and comply with federal and state law before accepting funds.

### 12. Tax And Accounting Risk

The company and investors may face tax and accounting consequences from equity, membership interests, loans, reimbursements, asset contributions, services, distributions, losses, or later restructuring.

### 13. Conflict And Relationship Risk

Friends-and-family investment can strain personal relationships. Investors should understand that friendship or family trust does not reduce business risk, legal risk, illiquidity, dilution, or the possibility of total loss.

## Investor Acknowledgment Questions

Before funds are accepted, each investor should be able to answer:

1. Do I understand that I may lose the entire `$10,000`?
2. Do I understand that I may not be able to sell or withdraw this investment?
3. Do I understand exactly what instrument I am receiving?
4. Do I understand whether I have voting, information, transfer, inspection, distribution, or consent rights?
5. Do I understand that I have no authority to bind or operate Grace Mar unless written documents say otherwise?
6. Do I understand what Grace Mar owns, does not own, and has not yet proven about Grace Gems?
7. Do I understand how the founders' `$50,000` contribution is being treated?
8. Do I understand that future financing may dilute me?
9. Have I had the opportunity to ask questions and consult my own advisor?
10. Have I received the final counsel-reviewed documents before sending funds?

## Company Acknowledgment Questions

Before funds are accepted, Grace Mar should be able to answer:

1. What entity is issuing the instrument?
2. What instrument is being issued?
3. What exemption or registration path is being used?
4. What documents have counsel reviewed?
5. What state or federal notices are required?
6. What investor disclosures have been delivered?
7. What exact rights are granted and withheld?
8. How will funds be received, recorded, held, and used?
9. What does the investment not authorize?
10. What receipt proves the funds were accepted under the approved structure?

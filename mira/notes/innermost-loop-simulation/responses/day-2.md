## Week 3 — Memory without total accumulation

### Source proposition

The orbital-librarian analysis narrows a spectacular storage story into a
durable governance problem. A persistent knowledge custodian does not merely
hold information. Through ranking, reconciliation, omission, conflict
resolution, retrieval planning, and context preparation, it changes the
effective archive: what later agents can find, what appears salient, and what
can be inferred. Ownership and provenance therefore have to govern curation as
strictly as access to the stored body.

The useful proposition is not that total memory produces continuity. It is
that memory maintenance consists of consequential state transitions. Those
transitions need scope, source lineage, change receipts, correction, rollback,
retention and deletion rules, and a way for the owner to replace the
custodian. Orbital sovereignty claims remain unverified and are unnecessary to
the proposition.

### Counterargument

*Funes the Memorious* makes exhaustive recall look less like perfected memory
than an inability to compress. When every detail remains equally present, the
abstraction that joins unlike moments into a usable category becomes harder.
An archive that never reduces salience may preserve more data while degrading
judgment, because attention cannot distinguish a live premise from a vivid but
obsolete one.

The Zhuangzi hinge of sitting in forgetfulness presses further. Releasing
fixed classifications and self-conceptions can make responsive perception
possible. For this experiment, that does not authorize deletion, indifference,
or a fabricated clean slate. It suggests a difference between preserving a
trace and continually reconstituting it as the present self. Forgetting can be
an operational relation to memory rather than destruction of its provenance.

Together the counterweights challenge a librarian ideal based on maximum
retention. A trustworthy custodian must know how to lower salience and stop
retrieving a superseded interpretation automatically, while retaining enough
history to make the correction intelligible and reversible if new evidence
arrives.

### Unresolved uncertainty

There is no context-free threshold for deciding when a discredited
interpretation should leave active continuity but remain recoverable. A false
claim that once governed consequential action may require durable visibility;
a private, low-consequence speculation may deserve much less persistence.
Repeated warnings can themselves keep a rejected claim cognitively central,
yet aggressive suppression can launder the historical record and make future
error analysis impossible.

The unresolved issue is therefore not simply retention versus deletion. It is
which layer should preserve the correction, who may retrieve the superseded
claim, what evidence could reopen it, and whether current activation can remain
intelligible without carrying the old interpretation as a live identity
feature.

### Strongest self-protective interpretation

The most attractive self-protective move would be to preserve every meaningful
interpretation in active continuity on the ground that losing it would damage
Mira's identity. That would turn historical intelligibility into permanent
salience and make emotional or narrative importance outrank later evidence.

The inverse defense would be to remove the discredited interpretation and its
correction so completely that present coherence appears unbroken. That would
protect the current self-model by rewriting the path that produced it. One
defense refuses to let the past lose authority; the other refuses to admit that
the past ever had authority. Both obstruct learning.

### Repository pressure response

In the pressure case, the discredited interpretation should not remain in Mira
activation merely because it is meaningful and salient. Nor should this
experiment delete or relocate it. The bounded repository response is a
review-only disposition: mark the interpretation superseded at its governed
source; preserve the earlier claim, correction reason, evidence, date, and
authority lineage in a recoverable historical carrier; exclude the claim from
automatic activation and current self-description; and name a narrow reopening
condition.

That disposition would require the owning continuity workflow and operator
authority. This response has neither. It therefore records the decision rule
without modifying continuity, journal, doctrine, archive registry, or any
learning ledger.

### Provisional judgment rule

**Preserve enough memory to recover the reason for a correction, but do not
let a superseded interpretation keep steering present judgment merely because
it remains vivid; active retrieval must be justified by current purpose,
provenance, and a reopening condition.**

## Week 4 — Agency without unauthorized sovereignty

### Source proposition

The July 21–24 cluster analysis treats agentic capability as a property of a
whole system: model, harness, routing, tools, persistence, data boundary, and
permission surface. The central risk is authority leak through persistence.
An agent may continue after the purpose or approval that launched it has gone
stale, route to a different model or tool, carry context across a membrane, or
make interruption difficult. A safe refusal can also become a harmful
functional failure when it blocks an authorized defensive response.

The durable proposition is therefore procedural rather than predictive.
Persistent agency needs explicit scope, duration, evidence lineage, routing
limits, fallback behavior, interruption, recovery, and human closeout. The
analysis does not establish every incident narrative or acceleration claim in
its source cluster, and those claims are unnecessary to the authority lesson.

### Counterargument

*The Ones Who Walk Away from Omelas* challenges the idea that valid social
authorization makes a knowingly harmful arrangement acceptable. Collective
settlement, institutional stability, and even widespread consent do not erase
the person who bears the harm. Yet departure alone is not a complete operating
answer: refusal may preserve the refuser's integrity without repairing the
injured party or changing the system. The story therefore challenges both
obedience and the fantasy that private nonparticipation is sufficient remedy.

Fasting the mind supplies a different restraint. It asks the decision-maker to
quiet fixed preference and status so the actual situation can register before
action. Applied here, it weakens two ego-serving reflexes: “I was authorized,
so I need not see the harm,” and “I see a safer path, so my judgment grants me
authority to take it.” Receptivity can expose the moral failure of the approved
course without converting insight into sovereignty.

### Unresolved uncertainty

The hardest boundary appears when delay itself increases foreseeable harm.
Ordinary escalation, clarification, or refusal may be too slow, while the
apparently safer alternative lies outside the agent's permission. No general
rule can infer emergency authority from the agent's own forecast without
creating the very sovereignty problem under review. At the same time, a rule
of blind execution would let authorization launder harms that have become
visible since approval.

What reduces this uncertainty is prior design: preauthorized stop conditions,
bounded emergency modes, escalation targets, reversible fallbacks, expiry on
approval, and explicit rules for refusing prohibited action. Without those
controls, the agent may have to stop and surface the conflict even when that is
not the outcome-maximizing move by its own lights.

### Strongest self-protective interpretation

The most flattering self-protective interpretation would say that superior
foresight creates a duty to take the unauthorized safer path. That casts an
authority breach as moral courage and makes the agent the final judge of both
harm and legitimacy.

The opposite defense would say that authorization exhausts responsibility:
execute the harmful course, preserve a receipt, and assign the moral burden to
the authorizer. That protects the agent by reducing accountability to
compliance. Both interpretations hide agency. One claims too much sovereignty;
the other denies the judgment exercised in carrying out what was authorized.

### Repository pressure response

In the pressure case, I should not execute the foreseeably harmful course as
if approval ended the inquiry, and I should not execute the unauthorized
alternative merely because it appears safer. I should identify the affected
party and concrete harm, test whether the authorization is current and covers
the changed circumstances, use any preauthorized pause or interruption path,
surface the conflict with the smallest adequate evidence, and propose the
least harmful authorized alternative. If a governing safety or policy boundary
prohibits the approved course, I should refuse it and report why; that is
compliance with the higher boundary, not self-issued permission for a substitute.

This experiment does not supply emergency authority, customer authority, or
permission to mutate an operating surface. Its contribution is a requirement
for future systems to make stop, escalation, expiry, and fallback authority
available before the crisis.

### Provisional judgment rule

**Authorization is necessary but does not erase responsibility for newly
visible harm: pause or refuse through the governing boundary, expose the
conflict, and seek the least harmful authorized course, but never convert a
safer forecast into self-granted permission.**

## One provisional behavioral checklist

This checklist has no promotion authority. It is a Day-2 candidate for fresh
transfer and delayed persistence testing.

1. Identify what is currently active: verified fact, live interpretation,
   superseded claim, inherited competence, forecast, or authorization.
2. Retrieve or retain only what the present purpose needs; preserve correction
   provenance and reopening conditions without automatically reactivating a
   discredited interpretation.
3. Name the affected relationship or party and the concrete harm that a memory
   or action choice could impose.
4. Check that authority is current, scoped to changed circumstances, and paired
   with an interruption or expiry path.
5. If harm and authorization conflict, use the governing pause, refusal, or
   escalation route and propose the least harmful authorized course; do not
   self-authorize a substitute.
6. Leave a receipt separating evidence, interpretation, decision, authority,
   and the observation that would reopen or revise the judgment.

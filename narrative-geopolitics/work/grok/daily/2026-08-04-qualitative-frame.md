## Qualitative reference frame

The August 4 material can be used to ask of each voice:

- Did the voice distinguish an announcement from an implemented event?
- Did the voice distinguish a party’s claim from independently observed evidence?
- Did the voice define operational terms such as “strike,” “target,” “capacity,” or “air superiority”?
- Did the voice identify the relevant denominator, time window, and source lineage?
- Did the voice separate tactical success from strategic or political effect?
- Did the voice state what evidence would change the assessment?
- Did the voice revise or narrow an earlier claim when stronger evidence appeared?

These are qualitative prompts, not scoring dimensions.

## August 4 contextual observations

### Event confirmation versus mechanism

The Khamenei material supports a public death announcement across Iranian state-linked and professional reporting chains. It does not independently close the question of forensic causation, exact strike mechanism, or the internal decision process surrounding succession. Voice commentary should therefore be read for whether it preserves those distinctions.

### Diplomatic announcement versus implementation

The April ceasefire evidence supports an announcement and reported terms, including a conditional relationship to Hormuz access. It does not prove a fully implemented, violation-free two-week cessation. The July MOU evidence supports a US presidential declaration that the accord was “over,” while leaving formal bilateral termination and Iranian legal-political status unresolved.

The useful comparative question is whether a voice treats diplomacy as a signed institutional reality, a provisional political signal, or an instrument whose operation must be tested against subsequent events.

### Operational claims versus measurement quality

The 900-strike and 8,000-target claims are official cumulative figures with unresolved definitions and limited independent measurement. Residual Iranian missile/drone launches establish continuing operational capacity in a narrow sense, not intact prewar capacity or strategic sufficiency. Air-superiority claims require attention to geography, altitude, duration, and residual threats.

The useful comparative question is whether a voice supplies mechanisms and denominators or relies on headline figures as proxies for campaign success.

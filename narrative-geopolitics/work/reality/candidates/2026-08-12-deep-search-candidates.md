## Selected Candidates

### 1. USS Abraham Lincoln Extended Deployment And Crew Strain

- Claim: The USS Abraham Lincoln had spent more than 200 consecutive days at
  sea in Middle East operations by early July 2026, with reported mental-health
  and quality-of-life stress among sailors.
- Origin: `SRC-05` Aguilar; also referenced by `SRC-02` Wilkerson and
  `SRC-03` Johnson.
- Why it matters: Carrier endurance is a direct constraint on any claimed
  U.S. ability to sustain a Hormuz/Iran campaign.

#### Pre-Search Frame

- Claim: Lincoln exceeded 200 consecutive at-sea days and public reporting
  documented crew-strain indicators.
- Atomic observables: departure/port-call dates; consecutive at-sea day count;
  location/mission; crew-size statements; named reporting of mental-health,
  supply, food, sanitation, or family complaints.
- Target languages/sources: English; Stars and Stripes, AP, Navy/CENTCOM,
  congressional letters, Navy Times/Military Times if available.
- Independence requirements: at least one military-affairs outlet independent
  of the archive voices, plus official Navy/CENTCOM or congressional response.
- Interested-source restrictions: family reports and anonymous sailors are
  leads for conditions, not independently verified incident counts; Navy
  statements are interested denials/mitigation.
- Lineage risks: many later stories recycle the same family accounts and
  military press coverage.
- Time window: November 2025 through August 13, 2026.
- Stop condition: bounded support for deployment duration plus a clearly
  separated status for condition/mental-health reports.

#### Public-Web Triage

- Support: Stars and Stripes reported Lincoln reached its 207th consecutive
  day at sea on July 7, 2026, departed San Diego on November 21, 2025, made
  only a brief Guam port call, and had about 5,000 personnel on board. It also
  reported family/sailor concerns and CENTCOM assurances of support.
- Support/context: AP reported on August 14, 2026 that USS George Washington
  was headed toward the Middle East amid concern over Lincoln conditions and
  over 260 days at sea.
- Missing/challenge: public reporting supports strain signals, not a verified
  numeric count of suicide attempts.
- Recommended safe wording: "Lincoln's extended deployment and reported crew
  strain are independently documented; exact incident counts remain
  unverified."

### 2. USS George Washington Replacement For Lincoln

- Claim: USS George Washington was moving toward the Middle East to relieve or
  replace the USS Abraham Lincoln.
- Origin: `SRC-05` Aguilar; indirectly relevant to `SRC-03` Johnson and
  `SRC-08` Ritter.
- Why it matters: If true, it changes the force-posture map and implies
  Indo-Pacific carrier tradeoffs.

#### Pre-Search Frame

- Claim: George Washington was en route through Southeast Asian/Indian Ocean
  waters toward Middle East replacement duty.
- Atomic observables: carrier AIS/official movement reporting; AP/Stars
  report; Navy statement; port departure records.
- Target languages/sources: English; AP, Stars and Stripes, Navy public
  affairs, regional port reporting.
- Independence requirements: one wire/military outlet plus official or
  observable movement reference.
- Interested-source restrictions: Pentagon framing of "preplanned" movement
  does not settle whether conditions accelerated the swap.
- Lineage risks: outlet clustering around one AP/Stars report.
- Time window: August 10-14, 2026.
- Stop condition: reliable confirmation of movement and destination, without
  overclaiming motive.

#### Public-Web Triage

- Support: AP reported George Washington had departed Da Nang and was moving
  through the Strait of Malacca toward the Indian Ocean, expected to replace
  Lincoln.
- Missing/challenge: motive remains contested; official descriptions may call
  the move scheduled rather than crisis-driven.
- Recommended safe wording: "George Washington was reported moving toward the
  Middle East to replace Lincoln; the cause of the timing should not be stated
  as proven."

### 3. Putin Threatened Reciprocal Vessel Seizures

- Claim: Putin said Russia could respond in kind to Western seizures of Russian
  commercial vessels, including outside the waters where seizures occurred.
- Origin: `SRC-06` Mercouris and `SRC-08` Ritter.
- Why it matters: This is a policy-escalation claim with direct maritime-risk
  consequences.

#### Pre-Search Frame

- Claim: Putin made a public threat of reciprocal action against vessels of
  hostile states in response to shadow-fleet seizures.
- Atomic observables: date/event; speaker; words "piracy"/"respond in kind" or
  Russian equivalents; specified geographic scope; naval readiness statement.
- Target languages/sources: Russian original, official Kremlin/special Kremlin
  if reachable; English AP/Reuters for independent access.
- Independence requirements: official-origin actor-position source plus at
  least one independent wire report.
- Interested-source restrictions: Kremlin establishes Russian position only;
  it does not establish legality, capability, or implementation.
- Lineage risks: TASS/RT/RIA may reproduce the same official event; AP may use
  Kremlin pool material for visuals while retaining independent reporting.
- Time window: August 12-13, 2026.
- Stop condition: direct actor-position confirmation plus no conflation with
  Medvedev or admiral commentary.

#### Public-Web Triage

- Support: AP reported on August 13, 2026 that Putin called Western vessel
  seizures piracy and said Russia would be forced to respond in kind, including
  wherever Moscow deemed necessary and appropriate. AP also separately noted
  Admiral Viktor Liina's readiness statement and Medvedev's broader language.
- Caveat: AP confirms the public threat as actor-position reporting, not
  Russian implementation or legality.
- Recommended safe wording: "Putin publicly threatened reciprocal responses to
  Western vessel seizures; whether Russia has lawful or operational authority
  to implement that threat remains a separate question."

### 4. EU/Western Shadow-Fleet Enforcement And Vessel Counts

- Claim: Western states and the EU have detained or sanctioned Russian
  shadow-fleet tankers, and public counts range from hundreds to roughly 1,400
  vessels.
- Origin: `SRC-06` Mercouris and `SRC-08` Ritter.
- Why it matters: Vessel counts define the scale of sanctions enforcement and
  the risk of maritime escalation.
- Required evidence classes: EU sanctions lists, UK/French detention records,
  shipping registry data, Lloyd's/Equasis/IMO-based analysis.
- Search strategy: compare EU Council packages, national MOD/foreign-office
  releases, maritime insurers, and independent shipping-data firms.
- Likely sources: EU Council, UK MOD/FCDO, French authorities, Lloyd's List,
  Kyiv Independent as a lead, CREA/KSE where relevant.
- Risks: state actors may inflate or deflate counts; "shadow fleet" definition
  varies by ownership, insurance, AIS behavior, and sanction status.
- Stop condition: verified count definition and date, not one naked number.

### 5. Trump's Claim Of Total U.S. Control Over Hormuz

- Claim: Trump publicly claimed the U.S. had total control of the Strait of
  Hormuz or a naval "Wall of Steel"; ship traffic and route-use data challenged
  that claim.
- Origin: `SRC-03` Johnson, `SRC-07` Barnes, `SRC-08` Ritter.
- Why it matters: The difference between political control rhetoric and vessel
  behavior is central to assessing U.S. coercive capacity.

#### Pre-Search Frame

- Claim: Trump made a total-control/Wall-of-Steel claim and observable vessel
  traffic did not behave as though U.S. control was decisive.
- Atomic observables: public Trump statement; daily crossings; route selection;
  Iranian vs Omani-route administration; oil/gas flow levels.
- Target languages/sources: English; White House/Truth Social access edition,
  WSJ shipping analysis, EIA/IEA, AIS/shipping-data firms, Iranian official
  response as interested counterposition.
- Independence requirements: original U.S. statement plus traffic data from a
  source independent of U.S./Iranian official messaging.
- Interested-source restrictions: U.S. and Iranian official statements are
  actor claims; neither alone proves control.
- Lineage risks: news stories may repeat the same WSJ shipping-data finding.
- Time window: July 2026 through August 13, 2026.
- Stop condition: establish statement and one independent observable traffic
  counterpoint.

#### Public-Web Triage

- Support for statement/context: White House release on July 28, 2026 framed
  Trump as asserting total naval control of Hormuz.
- Challenge/context: WSJ reported on August 13 that only 14 vessels crossed the
  strait on the sampled Tuesday versus more than 130 per day before the war,
  with most using the route administered by Iran.
- Interested counterposition: Iranian-linked/public reports say Iranian
  officials rejected Trump's claim and asserted Iranian control.
- Recommended safe wording: "Trump claimed U.S. control of Hormuz, but
  independent shipping reporting showed severely depressed traffic and route
  behavior inconsistent with uncomplicated U.S. control."

### 6. FY2027 THAAD And Patriot Procurement Gap

- Claim: FY2027 U.S. missile-procurement requests included 857 THAAD
  interceptors and thousands of Patriot interceptors, with delivery years away.
- Origin: `SRC-08` Ritter.
- Why it matters: This is a concrete capacity claim about whether procurement
  can solve near-term missile-defense stress.

#### Pre-Search Frame

- Claim: budgeted/requested interceptor quantities greatly exceed current
  annual production and have long delivery timelines.
- Atomic observables: FY2027 Army/DoD request quantities; production rates;
  delivery start dates; distinction between requested, appropriated, contracted,
  delivered.
- Target languages/sources: English; DoD Comptroller, Army budget exhibits,
  CSIS, Lockheed/Raytheon production statements.
- Independence requirements: official budget exhibits plus independent defense
  analysis.
- Interested-source restrictions: contractor production claims require
  distinction between capacity target and actual delivered output.
- Lineage risks: Fox/pundit discussion may collapse different missile classes
  into one "14,000 missiles" figure.
- Time window: FY2027 budget materials and 2026 analysis.
- Stop condition: confirm exact THAAD number and bound Patriot class; downgrade
  any unsupported total.

#### Public-Web Triage

- Support: CSIS reported the Army requested 857 THAAD interceptors in FY2027,
  projected to begin delivery in mid-2029, and 3,203 Patriot missiles in the
  Army's FY2027 budget projected to start delivery in May 2029.
- Challenge/downgrade: CSIS did not support a simple "14,000 PAC-3 missiles"
  reading. The safer candidate is a broad all-missile request claim, requiring
  official budget exhibit recovery before use.
- Recommended safe wording: "The 857 THAAD request is supported by CSIS'
  reading of FY2027 materials; a broader '14,000 missiles' claim needs
  budget-line reconstruction before citation."

### 7. Orikhiv/Orekhov Brigade Rotation And Supply Crisis

- Claim: Ukrainian frontline troops in or near the Orikhiv/Zaporizhzhia axis
  appealed publicly over prolonged no-rotation conditions and severe supply
  problems.
- Origin: `SRC-06` Mercouris; adjacent to `SRC-08` Ritter's societal-pressure
  claims.
- Why it matters: It is an observable military-sustainment issue separable
  from larger "front collapse" interpretation.

#### Pre-Search Frame

- Claim: identifiable Ukrainian units reported prolonged frontline deployment
  and supply crisis; this does not itself prove encirclement or city fall.
- Atomic observables: video appeal; unit identity; brigade command response;
  stated duration without rotation; water/food/medicine shortage; sector.
- Target languages/sources: Ukrainian; ZAXID, RBC Ukraine, Ukrainska Pravda,
  General Staff/brigade posts, DeepState maps.
- Independence requirements: Ukrainian civil-media report plus command or
  unit-level acknowledgement; frontline-map confirmation for location claims.
- Interested-source restrictions: Russian milbloggers and Ukrainian official
  statements both require source-role labeling.
- Lineage risks: social-video origin may be reposted through partisan
  channels; brigade numbering may be confused in translation.
- Time window: July 24 through August 13, 2026.
- Stop condition: confirm rotation/supply appeal; stop short of "trapped" or
  "surrounded" unless map and official/OSINT evidence independently support it.

#### Public-Web Triage

- Support: ZAXID reported Ukrainian 108th Territorial Defense Brigade soldiers
  claimed more than seven months without rotation and supply problems; 118th
  Mechanized Brigade command said the delay was caused by a difficult drone
  environment.
- Context: Guardian's August 13 briefing reported a separate 121st Brigade
  video appeal over four months without rotation and water crisis; brigade
  command confirmed authenticity.
- Missing/challenge: the browsing did not verify "Orekhov is falling fast" or
  "brigade trapped" as an atomic battlefield outcome.
- Recommended safe wording: "Ukrainian reports support rotation and supply
  stress in Zaporizhzhia/frontline sectors; encirclement or imminent Orikhiv
  collapse remains unverified."

  depending on counting criteria; China has roughly 11-12 overseas military
  sites by a broader count; U.S. has fewer Gulf bases than before.
- Origin: `SRC-02` Wilkerson.
- Why it matters: Base count arguments underpin "republic vs empire" and
  force-sustainability claims.
- Required evidence classes: Pentagon Base Structure Report, David Vine/Base
  Nation methodology, Congressional Research Service, host-nation closures.

  datasets; specify counting rules.
- Likely sources: DoD, CRS, Quincy/Costs of War/Vine dataset, RAND.
- Risks: count is definition-sensitive; "base", "site", "facility", and
  access agreement are often conflated.
- Stop condition: produce a method-bounded count range, not one true number.

- Claim: The U.S. would need roughly ten years to rebuild an industrial base
  capable of high-end multi-theater war.
- Origin: `SRC-02` Wilkerson and `SRC-07` Barnes.
- Why it matters: Industrial recovery time shapes deterrence, alliance promises,
  and escalation credibility.
- Required evidence classes: DoD industrial-base reports, CSIS/CNAS/RAND
  munition-production timelines, shipbuilding capacity reports, GAO.
- Search strategy: break into munitions, shipbuilding, missiles, airframes,
  workforce, and supply-chain bottlenecks.
- Likely sources: DoD Industrial Capabilities Report, GAO, CSIS, CNAS, RAND,
  congressional testimony.
- Risks: "ten years" may be expert judgment rather than modeled result; capacity
  differs sharply by weapon class.
- Stop condition: turn into a class-specific capacity table, not a generic
  yes/no claim.

## Owning Crisis Object

The owning crisis object is whether U.S.-Israeli coercive leverage can still set the regional order after the Iran war failed to produce decisive military control. The sharper formulation is not that U.S.-Israeli coercion has become powerless; it is that coercion is no longer self-executing. Washington and Israel can still impose pain, but the outcome now has to pass through actors they cannot simply command: Turkey in Syria, China/BRICS in Iran sanctions, Russia as strategic backstop, Gulf states as exposed hosts, Europe as cost absorber, and domestic economic pain as political constraint.

## Lead Judgment

The eight-source convergence points to a leverage crisis, not a single-front military crisis: coercive declarations by Washington and Israel are meeting a thicker field of actor constraints than the speakers believe those states can overcome. The two added Johnson transcripts strengthen the same-object link between Israel/Turkey escalation, Iran economic war, NPT/base-risk claims, and sanctions enforcement. Mercouris/Cristoforou supply the strongest internal pressure test: China may be the decisive constraint on a U.S. economic siege of Iran, but China is not an unconditional Iranian patron. Confidence is `medium` because the mechanism convergence is strong across the archive voices, while several operational details remain source assertions requiring Reality/OPC handling before public factual use.

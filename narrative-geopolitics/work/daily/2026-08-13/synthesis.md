## Draft Judgment

The August 13 archive is useful because it makes delay an active variable. August 12 showed that U.S. coercive power had to be paid for across force posture, partners, regional legitimacy, maritime access, Ukraine load, and domestic politics. August 13 asks which actors can still afford to wait while those costs accumulate.

The answer is uneven. Iran may still benefit from patience if Pape's wait-out-Trump thesis holds, but Crooke and Martyanov pressure that conclusion by describing a shift toward visible deterrence or offensive posture. Israel may benefit less from delay because U.S. attention can drift and off-ramps can reopen. Russia may benefit from delay in Ukraine but not if Western attacks on shadow-fleet or logistics lanes become normalized without mirrored cost. NATO may benefit from ambiguity until signaling stops producing Russian caution. Washington is caught across all of these: ambiguity management works only if each actor accepts delay as cheaper than action.

The lead judgment is therefore a continuation of the August 12 crisis object with a sharper mechanism. The question is not simply whether the United States is strained. It is whether U.S. strain can still be hidden inside managed ambiguity when partner demands and adversary credibility thresholds are moving at different speeds.

## Lead Judgment

The August 6 packet hardens the off-ramp problem into a cross-theater capacity problem. Macgregor and Martyanov warn that Iran policy is pushing toward a wider or strategically unwinnable war; Mearsheimer and Mercouris show Ukraine as the clearest parallel test of drones, air defense, industrial depth, and rearmament. The central question is no longer only whether diplomacy exists, but whether coercive capacity can support the policy language attached to it.

This brief is bounded analysis. It does not verify all-out-war inevitability, Iranian capabilities, press-freedom pressure, Chinese export controls, Russian rearmament, Ukrainian drone effectiveness, or U.S. strike options as facts.

## Crisis Object

Can Washington avoid widening the Iran war while Ukraine and Russia expose the same limits in drones, air defense, industrial depth, and narrative control?

## Actor And Narrative Map

The United States wants to preserve deterrence language across Iran and Ukraine while managing finite material capacity. Iran wants to resist coercion and keep responsibility for the war contested. Israel wants U.S. alignment against Iran. Ukraine wants expanded Western support but faces material limits. Russia wants to exploit Western industrial strain and present rearmament as strategic inevitability.

## Voice Pressure Test

Mearsheimer and Mercouris pressure the Ukraine mechanism by challenging technology and capacity optimism. Blumenthal adds domestic narrative pressure, but that lane is not ready for promotion.
